# NOTES — bilješke uz hrvatski prijevod (selah-hr)

*Ova datoteka bilježi stihove koje je dotaknula ljudska ruka ili pravilo —
da svatko tko provjerava neki stih vidi je li ga izradio stroj, ispravila
ruka ili odredilo pravilo, i zašto. Bilješke su na engleskom jer navode
hebrejsku morfologiju i jer je engleski radni jezik pravila zajedničkih
svim jezicima projekta Selah. Ništa se odavde tiho ne briše: bilješka se
ispravlja, a povijest izmjena dio je svjedočanstva.*

*Tekst je izradio stroj i čeka uho izvornoga govornika. Korpus se upravo
ponovno provjerava; kako i zašto, piše u `PROVENANCE.md`.*

---

## What these notes cover

The Croatian rendering was made on 2026-10-04 under the written discipline
`docs/methodology/translation-discipline/hr.md` (the Name **Jahve**,
declined on the stem; *Gospodin / GOSPODIN / Gospod / Jehova* and *Bog*
kept out of the Name's place; ijekavian standard Croatian; Serbian
ekavian forms and Serbian words forbidden; Hebrew-sound names). Entries
below follow the form *ref · what was done · why · date*.

## Verses corrected by hand

- **Ruth 4:15** · `למשיב נפש` rendered *za vraćanje duše* (restoring of
  the soul) · the machine wrote *vračanje* (sorcery) — one diacritic, č
  for ć — and wrote it again when the verse was rendered a second time;
  corrected by hand in both the flow and the gloss row. Naomi's
  *restorer of life* must not become witchcraft · 2026-10-04.
- **Ezekiel 1:21** · the machine wrote two attempts inside the flow,
  joined by *ispravljeno:* ("corrected:"), on three successive renderings;
  the machine's own corrected reading was kept and the first attempt and
  the joining word removed · 2026-10-04.
- **Jeremiah 48:41** · a bare ⟨ה⟩ in the flow dropped (Croatian has no
  article; ⟨⟩ carries ⟨את⟩ or a supplied Croatian word only) ·
  2026-10-04.
- **Isaiah 45:20** · a Cyrillic letter inside *Saberite* replaced with
  its Latin twin · 2026-10-04.

## Verses rendered again, and why

- **Haggai 2:21–23 · Psalm 40:5–8 · 2 Chronicles 29:6** · rendered in
  English (with ⟨the⟩, ⟨heavens⟩ in brackets); rendered again, now
  Croatian · 2026-10-04.
- **1 Samuel 28:13** · an English *of* in the flow; rendered again ·
  2026-10-04.
- **Isaiah 30:10** · `ולחזים` (and the seers) had been rendered *gatari*
  (diviners). The seers Israel silences are not diviners; rendered again
  as *vidjelacima … gledateljima* · 2026-10-04.
- **Numbers 10:14** · the Judah token `יהודה` carried a corrupted Hebrew
  surface reading `יהוה`, so the Name's place appeared where the text has
  Judah; the surface was restored from the source and the verse rendered
  again (*sinova Jehude*) · 2026-10-04.
- **2 Chronicles 34:24** · the Name's place was left empty; rendered
  again (*Tako reče Jahve*) · 2026-10-04.
- **Job 28:19** · a broken word row; re-serialized and rendered again ·
  2026-10-04.

## Mechanical corrections (whole corpus)

- **Cyrillic look-alikes inside Latin words** (*zlatа* with Cyrillic а,
  *bubregе* with Cyrillic е) replaced by their Latin twins; every swap
  checked Cyrillic → ASCII; the words themselves unchanged · 2026-10-04.
- **Hebrew surfaces corrupted by the target script** — a Hebrew letter
  replaced by a Latin, Cyrillic, Arabic or CJK character (`ש` → *š*,
  `ו` → *u*) — restored from the source text · 2026-10-04.

## Readings checked and kept as lawful

- **vrač / gatar** (diviner, sorcerer) stand only where the Hebrew speaks
  of divination and sorcery, and each such verse was read: Pharaoh's
  magicians `חרטמים` (Gen 41:24, Exod 7:11); the prohibitions (Lev 19:26,
  20:6; Deut 18); the diviners' oak `אלון מעוננים` (Judg 9:37); Endor
  (1 Sam 28); Balaam the diviner (Josh 13:22); Isa 2:6 · 3:2 · 8:19 ·
  47:9, 12 · 57:3; Jer 27:9 · 29:8; Mic 5:11; Zech 10:2; Daniel's
  Babylonian magicians (Dan 2:2 · 2:27 · 4:4 · 5:7). `נביא` is always
  *prorok*.
- **Words a pattern took for Serbian, and were Croatian**: *vremena* (an
  ijekavian genitive), *Bela* (Gen 36:32, a king of Edom), *idete*
  (which merely contains *dete*). A word lawful in the language cannot be
  convicted by a pattern that does not know the language.
- **gospodar / gospodaru moj** for a human lord (Gen 44:18, Judah to Joseph)
  is lawful; only the Name's place refuses the title.
- **bog / bogovi** in lower case for the gods of the nations and idols
  (Exod 32:8) is lawful; *Bog* in the place of `אלהים` is not.
- **`משיח`** for priest or king stands as *pomazanik* (anointed), per the
  discipline's D2.

## Open — awaiting a Croatian ear

- **Judges 9:37 · Micah 5:11** · *gatarâ* carries an archaic length mark;
  the word is lawful, the diacritic is odd.
- **Jeremiah 48:41** · the flow reads *Osuvojene*, the gloss *osvojene*;
  an odd verb form.
- **Psalm 40:5** · *blagožen* where *blažen* (blessed, `אשרי`) is
  expected.
- **2 Chronicles 29:6** · *vjerovalo prekršili* and *Jahve, Elohim
  našega* — the phrase and the case agreement want a native reading.
- **Genesis 41:24** · the gloss row repeats words across tokens
  (`אל` glossed *gatarima ⟨to⟩* beside `החרטמים` *gatarima*; `השבלים`
  *dobrih klasova* beside `הטבות` *dobrih*).
- **Ruth 4:15 and other verses** · the word row carries ⟨את⟩
  (`את שיבתך`) while the flow does not show the marker. Verses where the
  flow's ⟨את⟩ and the word row disagree were listed when the rendering
  was finished and are part of the present re-check.
- **Verses whose word row did not match the Hebrew tokens** (a row too
  many or too few), or whose ⟨⟩ held English, were taken out and are
  being rendered again now.
- **The discipline's own open questions**: does the Name decline
  naturally as *Jahvin*, *Jahvi*? Hebrew-sound personal names (*Avraham*,
  *Jicak*, *Jaakov*, *Moše*) or the traditional ones? The spelling of
  *ruah*, *hesed*, *cedek*, *Šabat*, *Miškan*.

## What the machine leaned toward

- **Its sister language.** Croatian and Serbian are close, and the
  machine's slips were toward Serbian: Cyrillic letters hidden inside
  Latin words, and a few whole Cyrillic words (*их*, *тама*). The Name
  itself held — *Jahve* in every place of `יהוה`, *Gospodin* nowhere in
  it.
- **One letter.** The worst single slip was a diacritic: č for ć turned
  restoring into sorcery (Ruth 4:15), twice.
- **English under strain.** A few short runs of verses came back in
  English, the machine's own working language.
